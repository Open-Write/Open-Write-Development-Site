# Voice Audition Guide — GOPC Part One

*12 main characters × 3 voice options each. 29 supporting characters × 1 voice option each.*

---

## How to Use

1. Each main character has three voice options (A, B, C) defined as MiMo-V2.5-TTS voice design prompts.
2. To audition a voice, run the smoke test against a sample line for that character.
3. Mark your selection in the table below. Once all main characters are decided, create `casting.json` from `casting.example.json` using only the winning voice keys.
4. Supporting characters have one voice each — review and adjust if needed.

## Main Characters — 3 Voice Options Each

### JOHN BROWN SR. (10 chapters — the protagonist)

| Option | Key | Voice Concept |
|--------|-----|---------------|
| **A** | `JOHN_BROWN_A` | **Prophetic authority.** Deep, resonant, deliberate. King James Scripture cadence absorbed through decades of reading aloud. Voice drops when pressurized. The authority of a man who believes God is listening. |
| **B** | `JOHN_BROWN_B` | **Frontier pragmatist.** Lower, rougher, more worn. Plain American speech with occasional biblical inflection breaking through. Controlled — the stillness is discipline, not calm. Prayer changes register entirely. |
| **C** | `JOHN_BROWN_C` | **Quiet intensity.** Not loud — the opposite of loud. Consonants precise, vowels short. When he reads Scripture, the room changes temperature. Dry humor deadpan. The warmth is in what he does not say. |

**Decision notes:** Option A foregrounds the scriptural/prophetic dimension. Option B grounds him in the frontier. Option C is the most interior — the stillness as coiled energy.

### OWEN BROWN (10 chapters — the son who wants to stay)

| Option | Key | Voice Concept |
|--------|-----|---------------|
| **A** | `OWEN_BROWN_A` | **Restless urgency.** Slightly rough, impatient. Body wants to move before mouth finishes sentence. Short clauses. Rising energy forced back down by father's presence. |
| **B** | `OWEN_BROWN_B` | **Quiet wanting.** Quieter than expected — a man who has learned his father's stillness is not his own. Sentences trail off when he realizes no one is listening. The son who stands in doorways. |
| **C** | `OWEN_BROWN_C` | **Practical frontier.** Direct, plain, no Scripture. The voice of a man who loads wagons and sharpens knives. Restlessness is in the pauses, not the words. |

**Decision notes:** Owen's defining quality is the wanting-to-stay, the doorframe moments. Option B foregrounds this. Option A gives him more active energy. Option C makes him more like his brothers.

### FRED BROWN (10 chapters — the deadliest Brown)

| Option | Key | Voice Concept |
|--------|-----|---------------|
| **A** | `FRED_BROWN_A` | **Precise watcher.** Low volume, few words. Each word chosen, placed. Stillness is attention, not discipline. Others register him before he speaks. |
| **B** | `FRED_BROWN_B` | **Barely there.** Voice recedes even as it speaks. Flat, almost affectless — but word choice reveals intelligence. Bible-reading voice is unexpectedly warm, clear, present. The contrast is the character. |
| **C** | `FRED_BROWN_C` | **Controlled capability.** Mid-low, decisive. The most capable person in any room. Sentences end decisively. Capability demonstrated, not described. |

**Decision notes:** Fred's power is in his silence. Option B's Bible-reading contrast is the most dramatic. Option A is the most economical. Option C foregrounds capability.

### JOHN BROWN JR. (9 chapters — the Inherited Manager)

| Option | Key | Voice Concept |
|--------|-----|---------------|
| **A** | `JB_JR_A` | **Institutional commander.** Clear, projecting. Governance vocabulary, complete sentences. Different instrument from father — designed for process, not prophecy. More formal under stress. |
| **B** | `JB_JR_B` | **Articulate builder.** Angular delivery — each clause a building block. Father's intensity in younger, more structured frame. Institutional confidence is real but brittle — cracks under pressure. |
| **C** | `JB_JR_C` | **Convention speaker.** Projecting, modulated, measured. The voice of Topeka convention. Formal even when exhausted. Father reads Prophets; son reads constitutions. |

**Decision notes:** JB Jr. is the institutional Brown. Option A is the most natural governance voice. Option B adds the brittleness that pays off post-Pottawatomie. Option C is the most public-facing.

### SAMUEL ADAIR (9 chapters — the man of peace)

| Option | Key | Voice Concept |
|--------|-----|---------------|
| **A** | `SAMUEL_ADAIR_A` | **Theological weight.** Minister's steady cadence — not revival thundering but quiet authority. Complete sentences, clauses that build. Never raises voice. Longer sentences when troubled. |
| **B** | `SAMUEL_ADAIR_B` | **Scholarly gentleness.** Measured, patient, diplomatic. Warmth is genuine — not performed piety. Prayer is private, conversational. Kansas wind in every sentence. |
| **C** | `SAMUEL_ADAIR_C` | **Plain principled.** Frontier theology strips decoration. Direct, clear, unflinching. When troubled: voice goes flat, ornament drops, bare sentences remain. |

**Decision notes:** Adair is the theological counterweight to Brown. Option A foregrounds the ministerial. Option B is warmer. Option C is the most stripped — pays off post-Pottawatomie when the theology falls away.

### ELIAS MORSE (10 chapters — the community anchor)

| Option | Key | Voice Concept |
|--------|-----|---------------|
| **A** | `MORSE_A` | **Commercial authority.** Steady, practical. Repeats customer's words back — buying time to calculate. Voice stays level when afraid; pauses get longer. |
| **B** | `MORSE_B` | **Quiet observer.** Sees everything from behind a counter, says less than he knows. Slight deliberateness from years of calculating change. Hands still = news matters. |
| **C** | `MORSE_C` | **Guarded merchant.** Warm but guarded — sells to both sides, trusts neither fully. Commercial rhythm. The Doubt in longer pauses. Always listening. |

**Decision notes:** Morse is the community's intelligence hub. Option A is the most commercial. Option B is the most interior. Option C foregrounds the dual loyalty.

### DR. LUCIUS VERUS GILL (7 chapters — the neutral ground)

| Option | Key | Voice Concept |
|--------|-----|---------------|
| **A** | `DR_GILL_A` | **Virginian clinician.** Low, clinical. Complete sentences for diagnosis, fragments when working. Never raises voice. Dry humor in worst moments. Quieter = more attention required. |
| **B** | `DR_GILL_B` | **Gentle physician.** Gentle hands in the voice. Measured, unhurried, almost musical. Neutrality is a choice, not indifference. Voice drops to near-whisper when troubled. |
| **C** | `DR_GILL_C` | **Rough frontier doctor.** Worn by Kansas wind and long rides. Virginian undertone in formal moments. Economy of instruction: 'Six stitches. Come back in a week.' |

**Decision notes:** Gill's neutrality is his defining quality. Option A foregrounds the Virginian. Option B is warmer. Option C is more frontier-worn.

### HENRY SHERMAN (7 chapters — Dutch Henry)

| Option | Key | Voice Concept |
|--------|-----|---------------|
| **A** | `HENRY_SHERMAN_A` | **Germanic plainness.** Accented, Germanic syntax. Thinks in German, translates. Quiet, practical. Haunted after loss — voice goes flat. |
| **B** | `HENRY_SHERMAN_B` | **Working steadiness.** Rhythm of dawn-to-dark labor. Accented but clear. Says what he means, stops. The immigrant who made it work. |
| **C** | `HENRY_SHERMAN_C` | **Bilingual depth.** Deeper, more reserved. Accent worn smooth. German with Katarina: warmer, quicker. English: cautious. The bilingual shift is the interior. |

**Decision notes:** Henry's immigrant identity is central. Option A is the most accent-forward. Option B is the most settled. Option C's bilingual shift is the most nuanced.

### KATARINA SHERMAN (7 chapters — "It is already here")

| Option | Key | Voice Concept |
|--------|-----|---------------|
| **A** | `KATARINA_SHERMAN_A` | **Sharp directness.** Accented, does not hedge. Names what Henry won't. German for important things. Authority of a woman with opinions inside her walls. |
| **B** | `KATARINA_SHERMAN_B` | **Warm knowing.** Warmer than sharp — but warmth doesn't soften directness. Low laugh at unexpected moments. Reads the German-language paper, knows which neighbors packed wagons. |
| **C** | `KATARINA_SHERMAN_C` | **Weighted prescience.** Lower register. Deliberate, with weight in every sentence. Has buried the idea that this will end well and is still setting the table. |

**Decision notes:** Katarina's prescience is her defining quality. Option A is the sharpest. Option B is the warmest. Option C is the most resigned — she already knows.

### RUTH BROWN ADAIR (9 chapters — the bridge)

| Option | Key | Voice Concept |
|--------|-----|---------------|
| **A** | `RUTH_ADAIR_A` | **Father's directness.** Plain, short, practical. 'Supper's at six.' Dry observation. Lower register. The bridge — Brown's steel, Adair's patience. |
| **B** | `RUTH_ADAIR_B` | **Competent warmth.** Efficient, not soft. Father's jaw, husband's theology, her own voice. When afraid: silent, works instead. Touches doorframes. |
| **C** | `RUTH_ADAIR_C` | **Quiet presence.** Quieter, more internal. Stands on porches watching roads. Pauses contain what she won't say. The doorframe touch is the voice's equivalent. |

**Decision notes:** Ruth bridges two households. Option A is the most direct. Option B is the most complete. Option C is the most atmospheric.

### MARY ANN DAY BROWN (3 chapters — the sentry)

| Option | Key | Voice Concept |
|--------|-----|---------------|
| **A** | `MARY_BROWN_A` | **Durable silence.** Plain. Fewer than ten lines in some of the most important scenes. Silence is her most powerful tool. When she speaks: weight of everything unsaid. |
| **B** | `MARY_BROWN_B` | **Surviving flatness.** Low, steady, flat — not affect but survival. Counts losses by moving fingers against skirt. Signs letters 'Your wife.' Questions contain their own answers. |
| **C** | `MARY_BROWN_C` | **Quiet authority.** Not bitter, not resigned — factual. The economy is earned. 'Come back to me' contains the entire marriage. |

**Decision notes:** Mary's power is in what she doesn't say. Option A foregrounds the silence. Option B is the most worn. Option C is the most dignified.

| Option | Key | Voice Concept |
|--------|-----|---------------|
| **A** | `VINCE_ANDERSON_A` | **Mannered courtesy.** Surprising vocabulary, soft, polite, controlled. Unsettling because the courtesy is the weapon. Lower register than expected for sixteen. |
